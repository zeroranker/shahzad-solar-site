The questions people actually ask

Answered straight, including the ones we cannot.

Eight questions that come up in almost every first conversation. Where the honest answer is "it depends" or "we do not know", that is what you will read. If your question is not here, ask it on WhatsApp and we will add it.

Can I get a zero bill with solar?

No, and anyone who promises you one is not telling you the truth. Once your own consumption is covered, fixed charges remain: roughly Rs 33 to Rs 35 per kW per month on a residential connection, and a minimum of about Rs 2,000 a month on an agricultural connection even at zero consumption.

A low bill, yes. A zero bill, no. What solar does is remove your purchase of power, not the charges for having a connection to the grid.

How long do solar panels actually last?

Panel warranties commonly run 25 years, and that number is broadly credible — it is a manufacturer warranty rather than a sales promise. In practice, degradation of roughly half a percent a year is normal, so a 25-year-old panel retains about 87% of its output.

Be more sceptical about the inverter. It is the component most likely to fail, its life is usually shorter, and it is where the warranty terms are worth reading closely. Ask for the warranty length in years, in writing, separately from the seller's own promise.

Who owns the net billing meter?

It depends on the size, and we are not going to invent a number for you. Small systems are normally metered with the distribution company's own meter; larger ones commonly involve a meter the consumer owns and pays for.

We have seen the 10 kW dividing line quoted often enough that it is worth naming plainly: we could not find it in S.R.O. 251(I)/2026. We read the gazette and it does not set that threshold. So treat 10 kW as something you have been told, not as a rule you have read, and get it confirmed. Ask your distributor which applies to your system in writing, before anything is installed.

How long does net billing approval take?

The regulations set minimums: acknowledgement within 5 working days, initial review within 15 days, and 7 working days each for concurrence and the connection letter.

Those are floors, not typical times. Real-world timelines in this market run two to four months and often longer, and a rejection restarts the clock. A check to ask before you order: is distributed generation on your transformer already at 80% of rated capacity? If it is, your application will not be entertained at all.

No one who is honest can promise you a date. We do not.

Should I buy a bigger system than my sanctioned load?

You cannot. Since 9 February 2026, Regulation 3(2) of the Prosumer Regulations caps your capacity at your sanctioned load. The old 1.5 times rule no longer applies.

Any quotation built on 1.5× was written before the change and is wrong. See the net billing page for the regulation and what it means for export.

Do solar panels work in Faisalabad's weather?

Faisalabad is a good location. Panels need bright sunlight, not heat — so actually they work slightly better on a cool bright day than a hot hazy one. The real limitations here are dust in summer and fog in winter.

A well-sited, regularly cleaned array produces roughly 4.2 kWh per kW per day, which is the figure we use throughout this site. Cleaning is a maintenance cost that belongs in your plan from day one, not an optional extra someone might mention later.

Do I need a battery?

Only if you use significant power after sunset. That is the whole test.

A battery's main value now is moving your own generated energy past the evening peak instead of exporting it at about Rs 11 a unit. If most of your consumption is in daylight — a shop, a daytime office, a well-scheduled pump — then a battery is a cost with very little return, and we will tell you that rather than sell you one.

Battery imports into Pakistan are up about 220% year on year, largely because scheduled load shedding returned in April 2026. That is a real reason to consider storage. It is not a reason to buy a battery you will not use.

Can I get money back from FESCO?

We do not know, and we are not going to tell you that we do. A refund or rebate on some part of an electricity bill is a thing governments do sometimes, and there is persistent talk of schemes tying solar to electric vehicles. But we could not find a current, primary source for any of it — no notification, no schedule, no terms.

So rather than repeat a rumour with a confident face, here is our answer: if someone has shown you a scheme in writing, ask FESCO directly and bring us the notification. If you cannot produce the document, it is not a scheme, whatever you have been told.

What is the actual difference between net metering and net billing?

The name changed, and so did the money. Under net metering your exported power was bought back at your full retail tariff — the same price you paid for it. Under net billing it is priced at the national average energy purchase price, which is roughly a quarter of the residential rate.

That is the whole change. The system on your roof does not care what the rules are called, but your bill does. See the full explanation.

I already have solar. What happens to me?

Your agreement and its term survive. Regulation 21(2) keeps them alive. Your price does not survive with them: the proviso is explicit that existing distributed generation "shall be billed in accordance with the national average power purchase price till the expiry of the term of their agreement."

So if anyone has told you that an old connection protects your export rate, that is not what the gazette says. More here, including the part about S.R.O. 547(I)/2026 that we could not read.

Is it correct to call an installer "AEDB certified"?

Usually it is a misuse, and the distinction matters enough that we publish our own entry in full. The AEDB registers certified installers in three categories with different capacity ceilings. Being on a list is not the same as holding a valid certificate today, and the public list is not filtered by validity date.

We show ours verbatim, including the parts that are not flattering. Read the record.

Do electricity tariffs have fixed charges as well as unit rates?

Yes, and this is the single most common misunderstanding we encounter. A tariff is not one number. You pay a rate per unit and a fixed charge for having the connection at all, which does not fall when your consumption falls.

On a residential connection the fixed charge is roughly Rs 33 to Rs 35 per kW per month. On an agricultural connection there is a minimum of about Rs 2,000 a month even at zero consumption. This is why we will not promise a zero bill to anyone.

Should I enlarge the system I already have?

Sometimes, and the deciding question is not capacity — it is whether the extra generation lands on a load that is running at that hour. A bigger array on a building whose demand has not grown mostly produces more export, and export is now the cheap thing.

The protection under Regulation 21(2) attaches to the capacity you already had, so enlarging is a genuinely different decision from installing for the first time. Work out the new shape before you commit, not just the new size.

How do payments work?

Our terms are 30 / 40 / 30:

  • 30% — detailed price quote on letterhead with our NTN, before work starts.
  • 40% — when the material reaches site.
  • 30% — after commissioning and handover, once the system has been shown to you working.

The last third is held back deliberately, so that our money and yours finish on the same day. Each instalment's warranty card is registered in your name. If a quotation asks for more up front than this, ask why — it is check number seven.

The ones we cannot answer

Four things we genuinely do not know.

A FAQ that only contains answers we like the sound of is marketing. These are the questions we could not resolve from a primary source, and we would rather say so than improvise.

Is your AEDB certificate currently valid?

The public register shows a validity date of 8 June 2025, and the list itself was published in April 2026 with that date still showing. It may have been renewed. We will not claim either way. The full record is on our about page.

What does S.R.O. 547(I)/2026 actually say?

It is known to bear on grandfathering of pre-February connections, but it is published as an image-only scan and we could not read its text. We therefore do not summarise it. If your existing system is pre-February, ask us and we will tell you what we actually know.

What is NEPRA's actual determination on the export rate?

The gazette sets a formula — the national average energy purchase price — not a number. The "about Rs 11" in widespread circulation is a Power Division statement, and Regulation 14(3) allows the rate to be revised during your agreement's life. Treat Rs 11 as the best current estimate, not a fixed price.

What is the status of the Punjab Solar Tubewell Scheme for 2026?

We could not confirm it from a primary source, so we do not state it. The CM Punjab Free Solar Scheme's registrations are closed — that we can confirm. See the farms page.
