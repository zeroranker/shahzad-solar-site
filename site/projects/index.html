Rooftop · Commercial

70 kW, Faizan-e-Madina

A commercial rooftop array in the city, published in May 2025. Worth watching alongside the hospital job: it is a smaller system on a building that genuinely uses power during trading hours.

That matters more than it used to. Under net billing, a commercial site with daytime consumption keeps a much higher share of what it generates — the power is used on site at the full commercial tariff rather than sold at about Rs 11. (That Rs 11 is a Power Division figure reported in the press, not a number written in the gazette, which fixes the formula only — see the full explanation.) It is the profile the new rules reward.

Ground-mounted · Industrial · Top of our C-3 range

250 kW, Faisalabad

A ground-mounted industrial array at exactly the top of the 250 kW range on our AEDB C-3 certificate: 250 kW. If you are weighing a rooftop against a ground mount, this is the video to ask questions about.

One rule to know if you are considering something this size: from 250 kW, a load-flow study is compulsory before approval, carried out through the distribution company or a consultant registered with the Pakistan Engineering Council. At exactly 250 kW you are on the threshold, so plan for it in your timeline and your budget.

The corrections

Two claims we published that the footage did not support.

Both of these were found by actually watching our own videos, which is the argument for linking to footage in the first place.

The "weaving factory" is not a weaving factory

We published a video titled "Weaving factory, Faisalabad". The footage shows a small rooftop array of roughly twelve panels on a residential-style flat roof. It is not a factory, and it is not in any meaningful sense an industrial installation.

The video also carries an on-screen figure of 106,543.5 kWh annual generation. That is not physically possible for an array of that size: twelve panels cannot generate that much energy in a year. The figure is wrong.

We have removed this from our home page and do not present it as a project. It remains described here, in the open, because deleting a wrong claim quietly and saying nothing teaches nothing.

See the footage for yourself ↗

The second correction is smaller but worth stating. One of our videos shows a payback figure of ten months. We have not repeated that number anywhere on this site, because a payback claim depends on the tariff, the load, the installed cost and the export price — and under net billing the export price is the thing that has just changed most. If you see a ten-month payback quoted to you in 2026, ask which of those four assumptions it depends on.
