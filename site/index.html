On 9 February 2026 Pakistan replaced net metering with net billing. You still buy power at your full tariff, but any power you export is now bought at roughly Rs 11 a unit instead of cancelling your bill one for one. 05 — Straight answers

The questions we would rather you asked in person.

Will my bill go to zero?

No. Anyone who tells you it will is telling you something false. Fixed charges still apply — roughly Rs 33 to Rs 35 per kW per month, with monthly minimums around Rs 75 to Rs 150 for a home, and Rs 2,000 a month on an agricultural connection even at zero consumption. You can cut a bill a long way. You cannot remove it.

How long until the system is approved?

Your distribution company decides, not us, and we will not promise you a date. The rules set minimum statutory steps — five working days to acknowledge, an initial review within fifteen, then seven each for concurrence and for the connection letter. Those are floors, not typical elapsed times. Reported real-world timelines in this market run two to four months, and rejection restarts the clock.

What size system should I buy?

The smallest one that covers what you use in daylight, up to your sanctioned load. Regulation 3(2) now caps a facility at your sanctioned load, so the 1.5× sizing that used to be legal is not. The slider tool above will show you what a given size does on your own numbers, and it will tell you when you have made it too big.

Do you offer instalments?

No. We do not lend and we do not arrange third-party finance. Our structure is 30% against an itemised quote and an NTN invoice, 40% on delivery of equipment, and 30% after commissioning and handover. If you need a loan to buy solar, that is a separate conversation and we will not pretend otherwise.

What happens if it underperforms?

It should be a clause in the contract, not a conversation afterwards. We put it in the quotation before you pay anything. If a supplier will not put a performance commitment in writing, that is the most useful thing you can learn about them before you sign, and it costs you nothing to ask.

Are you actually certified?

Here is the awkward version. We appear in FESCO's published register of AEDB certified installers, category C-3, certificate CR/24/027/C-3, entry 16. The validity date on that certificate has passed, and the list was published with that date still showing. We print it exactly as it stands rather than describing ourselves as currently certified when we cannot prove it. The whole record is on our about page, including the part that is inconvenient for us.
