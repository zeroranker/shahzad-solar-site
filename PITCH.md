# The pitch — Shahzad Solar

Everything here is grounded in the client's own public record and the gazette.
Every number in the pitch is traceable to a source listed at the end. Do not
add a figure to this document that is not in that table.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

> On 9 February 2026 the rules for selling your solar changed. Exported power
> used to be paid at your full tariff — Rs 47.20 a unit. Under the new net
> billing rules it is paid at roughly Rs 11. That means the biggest system you
> can afford is no longer the one that earns you the most, and almost nobody in
> Faisalabad has explained this properly. We built you a website that does —
> in English and Urdu — and that shows your own clients their own numbers
> rather than a generic savings claim. It is finished, it works in both
> languages, and you can read every number on it and check where it came from.

## 2. What to send, in order

**Message 1 — WhatsApp, the opener.** Keep it under 60 words. Do not describe
the design. Do not describe the features. Describe the problem.

> Assalam-o-alaikum. I am [name]. I looked at Shahzad Solar because you are
> listed in the AEDB installer register for Faisalabad, and because the net
> billing rules changed in February and most of your competitors have said
> nothing about it.
>
> I have already built something. Can I show you two minutes of it on video
> call, or would you rather I send a link?

**Message 2 — the follow-up, if they say "send a link".**

> Of course. Before I do, one thing worth knowing: the site does not hide the
> parts that are not flattering. Your certificate date on the register has
> passed and the list is not filtered by validity, so we say so on the About
> page instead of hoping nobody checks. If that is a problem, tell me now.

That second message is the whole pitch. It is the one thing no competitor can
send, because it is only true if you actually did the verification.

---

## 3. The call script

### Opening — earn the right to continue (30 seconds)

> "Before I show you anything — do you know what changed on 9 February?"

Most installers do not. Say the sentence that follows:

> "The government replaced net metering with net billing. Under the old rules
> you sold your extra electricity back at your full tariff — forty-seven
> rupees twenty a unit. Under the new rules you sell it at roughly eleven.
> That is the national average energy purchase price, and it is a formula,
> not a fixed number, so NEPRA can revise it during your agreement."

**Then stop.** Let them react. If they say "we knew that", ask what they tell
customers who ask whether to go bigger. That question is the entire meeting.

### The core demonstration — show, do not tell (3 minutes)

Open the home page. Do not scroll past the hero.

> "This is the model your customers will actually use. Five kilowatts, thirty
> days — it makes six hundred and thirty units. Eighty-eight used on site,
> five hundred and forty-two sold. And here is what that is worth now: ten
> thousand one hundred and thirty-one rupees a month, against twenty-nine
> thousand seven hundred and thirty-six under the old rules."

Then the part that lands:

> "That is a system that makes the customer worse off than they were before,
> and it is telling them so. Watch — if I drop the size to two kilowatts, the
> verdict changes, because now it is matched to what they use. Same tool, same
> company, opposite conclusion."

**Why this is the demonstration:** it proves the tool is not an advert. An
advert has one answer. This has an answer that depends on the customer.

### The proof of work — show the register (2 minutes)

Open /about/. Read the entry out loud, exactly as it appears on screen.

> "This is your entry in the AEDB register, copied from the PDF FESCO
> publishes. Note the date: June 2025. That has passed. Most sites would have
> hidden this. We print it, and then we tell you what to ask the authority to
> confirm it."

If they react well to that, you have won. If they react badly, you have learned
something important about whether this client can be worked with.

### The second signature — the checklist (1 minute)

Open /before-you-buy/.

> "Twelve checks. Your customers run it against you. Two of them are
> uncomfortable — one asks who holds the warranty card in their own name, and
> one asks to visit a running installation and ring the owner. We published it
> anyway, because a checklist that only flatters you is worth nothing."

### Close (30 seconds)

> "It is built, in English and Urdu, on your own project footage, with your
> real address and numbers. Two things I need from you: whether that
> certificate has been renewed, so I can replace the disclosure with the fact —
> and permission to go live."

## 4. Objections, answered

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We already have a Facebook page with 100,000 followers." | "Yes, and Instagram has 474. That ratio is not real reach. That is why we did not put follower counts anywhere on the site — a customer who checks will find it and it damages you." |
| "Send us a quotation first." | "Of course. Send me the monthly bill and the sanctioned load, and I will send the worked example first. You will see the site computes the same thing on the phone." |
| "We cannot say the certificate expired, our customers will leave." | "Your competitors already know. A customer who finds it on the register after installation is worse than one who was told up front. On the site they find it before they pay you anything." |
| "Why Urdu and English?" | "Eighty-two percent of your net-metered connections are ten kilowatts or under. That is households. And most of the people who read the rule change will read it in Urdu." |
| "The export rate of eleven is only a statement, not law." | "Correct, and the site says exactly that, with Regulation 14(3) quoted beside it because NEPRA can revise the price. It is a formula, not a number. We do not pretend otherwise." |
| "What if a customer says your tool told them not to buy?" | "Then they buy a matched system instead of an oversized one, they do not feel misled, and you hear about it before it becomes a warranty dispute. That is a cheaper outcome than the alternative." |
| "Who made this? How do we know it is real?" | "It is on GitHub in public, with the gazette PDFs the whole thing rests on, and the scripts that check it. Send them to your engineer." |

## 6. If they ask what to do next

1. Confirm the certificate status. One phone call to the AEDB desk. It changes
   the About page from a disclosure into a claim.
2. Confirm the address form they want printed, and whether "near Rescue 1122"
   and "Afghanabad Road" are how their own customers find them.
3. Confirm whether the 1,000 kW Awami Paper Mill figure should stand, and
   whether any document can support it.
4. Send three project videos they are happy to have linked permanently.
5. Give permission to go live under the Vercel URL, or buy `shahzadsolar.pk`
   and point it at Vercel.
