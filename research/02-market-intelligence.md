# Market Intelligence — Pakistan & Faisalabad Solar
Compiled 2026-09-30. Every figure carries a date and a source. Old and new data are never blended.

---

## 1. THE REGULATORY RUPTURE — the single most important fact in this market

**NEPRA (Prosumer) Regulations, 2026 — SRO 251(I)/2026, notified 9 February 2026.**

| Change | Old (net metering, 2015 regs) | New (net billing, 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Basis of credit | 1:1 retail offset — every exported kWh cancelled a kWh of import at full retail rate | Export bought at the **national average energy purchase price** (~Rs 10–11/unit) |
| Retail tariff for comparison | ~Rs 45–55/unit | unchanged — you still pay full retail to import |
| Agreement term | 7 years | **5 years** |
| Max array size vs sanctioned load | **1.5× sanctioned load** | **1.0× sanctioned load** (Reg 3(2)) — *arguably a bigger shock than the export rate* |
| Max distributed generation | effectively sanctioned load | **1 MW** hard cap (Reg 2(1)(ix)); applicant must be 3-phase 400V/11kV or single-point bulk |
| Transformer headroom | — | **New applications barred once DG on that transformer hits 80% of rating** (Reg 3(5)) |
| Systems ≥ 250 kW | — | **Mandatory load-flow study** (Reg 3(3)), via licensee or PEC-registered consultant |
| NEPRA concurrence fee | — | **Rs 1,000/kW, non-refundable** — *removed for ≤25 kW by the 6 Aug 2026 amendment* |
| Interconnection cost | — | **Prosumer bears all**, incl. metering and any upgrade needed solely for the connection (Reg 12) |
| Rate certainty | fixed 7-yr contract | **None.** Reg 14(3) lets NEPRA revise the export rate *during the life of an agreement*, deemed incorporated |

**The export rate is a formula, not a number.** The regs specify the *national average energy purchase
price* (NAEPP), not a fixed figure. **~Rs 11/kWh** is the figure from the **Power Division via The News,
10 Feb 2026** — the most authoritative located. ⚠️ Do not confuse it with **NAPP** (national average
*power* purchase price, ~Rs 27/kWh), which is what grandfathered prosumers keep. **Three different
numbers circulate in the market (Rs 8.13, Rs 11, Rs 13); Rs 13 is a superseded 2025 draft figure still
being served by SEO sites two years later.**

**Grandfathering — 9 February 2026.** The operative date is **9 Feb 2026**, the date of SRO 251(I)/2026.
The final amendment of **2 April 2026** (retrospective to 9 Feb) confirms that approvals, licences and
agreements secured before that date remain valid on old rates to contract expiry. ⚠️ **A widely repeated
"8 February" cutoff could NOT be verified in any instrument** — it likely derives from a press
headline about a pre-deadline clearance push. **Use 9 February. Treat 8 February as unverified.**

**The grandfathering cliff nobody explains:** under the April amendment, *any modification that
increases maximum electrical output forfeits the old tariff advantages.* A grandfathered prosumer who
added capacity "to be safe" may already have lost protection — and is now **locked at their existing
system size**.

**Further amendment — S.R.O. 1330(I)/2026, 6 August 2026 (full text verified).** Its consequential
provision: **a prosumer with a distributed generation facility of 25 kW or below no longer needs NEPRA
concurrence — the licensee shall accord approval.** This is the single biggest recent change for the
residential and small-commercial market that dominates Faisalabad's 27,490 net-metered connections.

**Court challenge — resolved, no injunction.** A Lahore High Court petition (Syed Azhar Hussain, before
Justice Abid Hussain Chattha, filed 13 Feb 2026) argued fundamental and property rights, and that
buying high / selling low is discriminatory. On **24 Feb 2026** the Ministry of Energy directed
billing to continue under the 2015 regulations, the petitioner **withdrew**, and the case was
**disposed of on withdrawal**. No merits judgment; the arguments were never adjudicated.

**FESCO implementation is fragmented — a real, documented source of confusion.**
- **PITC runs the national Net Billing Portal** (nmp.pitc.com.pk) for all ten DISCOs including FESCO,
  with "Apply For Net Billing (By Installer)" and "(By Consumer)" forms.
- FESCO users seeking an **Extension of Load** must instead use a **separate ENC portal**
  (fesco.enc.com.pk). **Two different systems for one transaction.** This is a concrete, citable cause
  of dropped applications and mis-selling — and a genuine service a good installer should manage.
- ⚠️ **No DISCO-level published net-billing rate schedule was found. No FESCO "solar hours"
  announcement exists** — searched specifically. Their absence is itself a finding.

Sources: The News 2026-02-09 · The News 2026-04-02 · Arab News 2026-02-10 · Energy Update 2026-07-18 ·
pv magazine 2026-08-19.

### The commercial consequence
**Pure export is now uneconomic.** A system sized larger than the site's daytime load earns roughly
Rs 10–11 per exported unit instead of Rs 45–55. The correct design is now **self-consumption-led**:
right-size the array to the load that actually exists during daylight hours, minimise export, and add
storage where the value is in the evening peak. Under the old rules, oversizing was rewarded. It no
longer is. **This inverts the single most common sales argument in the Pakistani solar market.**

This matters most for exactly the customers Faisalabad has: three-shift textile, weaving and dyeing
loads with enormous daytime demand and therefore very little export.

## 1b. FESCO TARIFFS — the numbers a Faisalabad customer actually pays
**Source: FESCO's own published schedule, effective 12 February 2026 — `fesco.com.pk/tariff`.**
⚠️ FESCO publishes two columns: **C** = uniform *determined* tariff (general rate) and **D** =
**"GoP applicable variable charges"** — column **D** is the operative rate a customer actually pays.
SEO sites routinely quote the wrong one. Figures below are **column D, Rs/kWh**.

| Category | Rate |
|---|---|
| Residential — **protected** 001–100 | 10.54 |
| Residential — **protected** 101–200 | 13.01 |
| Residential — un-protected 001–100 | 22.44 |
| Residential — un-protected 101–200 | 28.91 |
| Residential — un-protected 301–400 | 36.46 |
| Residential — un-protected 601–700 | 41.85 |
| Residential — un-protected **>700** | **47.20** |
| Residential ToU (≥5 kW) peak / off-peak | 46.85 / 34.53 |
| Commercial <5 kW / ≥5 kW | 37.44 / 39.76 |
| **Industrial B1 ≤25 kW** | **26.23** |
| **Industrial B2(a) 25–500 kW** | **24.16** |
| Industrial ToU peak / off-peak | 35.74–36.68 / 25.48–27.41 |
| **Agricultural tubewells** | **28.90** — the cheapest non-protected category in the entire schedule |

Fixed charges: residential **Rs 33.09–35.03 per kW per month** (changed from a flat Rs/consumer/month);
minimum Rs 75 single-phase, Rs 150 three-phase; **agriculture Rs 2,000/consumer/month even at zero
consumption**. A "zero bill" is therefore arithmetically impossible for almost every category.

**Corrections to widely repeated claims, verified against FESCO's own two schedules:**
- ❌ "Tariffs jumped in February 2026." **Not supported.** Variable rates were broadly flat to *lower*.
  Industrial B1 fell 30.50 → 26.23 (−14%); B2(a) fell 30.73 → 24.16 (−21%). What changed is the
  **fixed-charge architecture** and the wider ToU off-peak differential.
- ✅ FY2020→FY2024 tariffs rose ~155%, averaging ~**Rs 44/kWh** (Renewables First, 10 Sep 2026).
- ⚠️ FESCO's published record has a gap: **11 Jan → 12 Feb 2026 is unaccounted for.**

**Protection is worth more than the tariff alone.** Unprotected residential at >700 units pays
**Rs 47.20** — **3.6× the protected 001–100 rate of Rs 10.54**. Under net billing there is a real risk
that cutting grid consumption changes a consumer's protection status. An academic review (Yousuf,
*Next Energy*, 2026) flags exactly this as an unaddressed design flaw. **This is the single most
useful thing a Faisalabad solar company can tell a careful customer.**

## 1c. LOAD SHEDDING IS BACK — 2026's biggest reversal
**14 April 2026: the Power Division announced scheduled load shedding of ~2.5 hours daily, 5:00 PM to
1:00 AM**, and warned tariffs may rise further. Other 2026 reports cite up to 2 hours daily.
Mechanism: a **>4,000 MW demand–supply gap** from plants short of imported LNG after the 2026
Strait of Hormuz disruption, with furnace-oil and diesel backup too costly to run.
*Source: PakSpectrum 2026-04-14. ⚠️ feeder-level hour tables circulating online are SEO content, not
DISCO publications — treat all of them as unverified.*

**Why this matters commercially:** it reverses the 2025 "solar solves everything" narrative and
strengthens the **solar-plus-storage** case rather than the solar-only case. Battery imports rose
**220% year-on-year to 4.6 GWh in 2025** (Renewables First); ~282,000 solarised Pakistani households —
about 1 in 26 — now have storage, concentrated in the 5–10 kWh band.

## 1d. TAXES AND PRICES — 2026 actually favoured the buyer
- **Panels: 0% customs duty, GST held at 10%.** The proposed **18% GST hike was DROPPED** from the
  final 2026-27 Finance Bill. The 18% push, which would have added ~Rs 7,500–9,000 per panel before
  being withdrawn, was politically sponsored by the domestic panel-manufacturing lobby.
- **Module landed cost is UP, not down: ~$0.09/W (2025 average) → ~$0.11/W (2026 YTD), +25% YoY** —
  a rupee-depreciation story, and therefore the main price risk (PSA consignment data).
- 600 W+ modules went from 42.1% of 2025 imports to 55.8% of 2026 imports.
- **Diesel crossed PKR 300/litre** (cut Rs 1.96 for 22 Sep 2026) — the number that decides
  tubewell economics.

## 1e. AGRICULTURE — the structurally advantaged segment
- **FESCO agricultural tubewell tariff Rs 28.90/kWh — the cheapest non-protected category in the
  whole FESCO schedule**, below every residential slab including the top protected one, and
  **unchanged** between the July 2025 and February 2026 tariffs.
- **Punjab Solar Tubewell Scheme 2026: Rs 15 billion in the Punjab Budget 2026-27, ~8,000 units, up to
  80% subsidy**, applications via `agriculture.punjab.gov.pk`. *(Two sources agree; verify against the
  Punjab Finance Act 2026-27 before publishing.)*
- ~50,000 solar tubewells exist across Punjab and Sindh. Diesel tubewells reportedly cost 2–3× more
  per cusec *(vendor source — unverified)*.
- **The key analytical point: tubewells are a pure off-grid/standby use case, never dependent on net
  metering, and therefore structurally immune to the net-billing change. Lowest policy risk in the
  entire market.**

> ⭐ **The single most important structural fact in this market:**
> **466,000 net-metered connections vs 7.3 million solarised households — roughly 6%.**
> Any analysis built on NEPRA's registry is looking at a small minority of the real fleet. That gap is
> the root cause of the policy error itself. It is also why "the market" and "the regulated market"
> behave differently, and why a Faisalabad installer talking to a homeowner is often talking to
> someone the regulator has never heard of.

**The 2025 import "collapse" was a pull-forward artefact, not a demand collapse.** PSA's own analysis:
monthly imports peaked at **4.99 GW in April 2025** and fell to 0.22 GW in November and 0.13 GW in
December. PSA attributes the spike to cheap panels **plus "growing signals that NEPRA was preparing to
overhaul its net metering rules, enough on its own to send buyers rushing to purchase panels and lodge
applications before any new framework took effect."** PSA: *"The market is not in retreat. It is in
transition."*

**Module prices are UP, not down:** ~$0.09/W (2025 avg) → **~$0.11/W (2026 YTD), +25% YoY**, driven by
rupee depreciation. 600 W+ modules rose from 42.1% to 55.8% of imports.

## 4. DOCUMENTED TRUST FAILURES — what customers are actually afraid of

Practitioner account naming Faisalabad directly (solarz.pk, 2026-04-23), plus press:

1. **Fake system capacity.** Sold "10 kW", delivered ~7.2 kW. Buyer check: *panel count × wattage*.
2. **Low-quality panels sold as "Tier-1."** B-grade, refurbished, unknown brands. The source is
   explicit: ***"'Tier-1' is not a certification — it's often used loosely in Pakistan."***
3. **Misleading net-metering promises.** "Approval in 2 weeks"; "we have connections inside the
   department." Real timelines are **2–4 months or longer**; DISCO-driven.
4. **Disappearing installers after advance.** 50–70% taken, installation delayed, contact lost.
5. **Cheap inverters with no warranty.** *"In Faisalabad (D-Ground), a client's inverter failed within
   three months — and there was no support."*
6. **"Zero bill" is a lie.** Fixed charges of Rs 2,000–4,000 still apply; winter output falls.
   A 5 kW system ≈ 600–750 units/month summer, 350–450 winter.
7. **Overpricing behind the word "premium."** 10 kW actually costs Rs 1.3–1.6M; quoted Rs 1.8–2.2M.
8. **Brand switching.** Dealers change panel brand at the last minute if prices move.
9. **Undersizing on purpose**, knowing the client will not notice.

**Scale of the problem (Senate panel, Feb 2026):** Rs 110 bn of fraud/money laundering in solar
imports across 80 companies, some fake; FBR flagged Rs 69 bn in over-invoicing, filed FIRs against 13
companies, and found Rs 18 bn illegally transferred abroad. Source: The Friday Times 2026-02-18.

**DISCO behaviour (TechJuice, 2026-01-28):** distributors reportedly excluding solar-generated units
from bills; a 22 kW approved DG consumer had the *entire* export discarded rather than capped;
thousands with signed contracts and paid demand notices waiting on meters that never arrived.

**Market price reference, 2026 (practitioner estimate — treat as a range, not a quote):**
5 kW Rs 750,000–900,000 · 10 kW Rs 1.3M–1.6M · 15 kW Rs 2M–2.4M.

**Buyer-protection norm the market already knows:** 30% advance / 40% on delivery / 30% after
commissioning. Demand: NTN invoice, warranty cards, net-metering documents. Visit at least one live
site; speak to an actual customer.

## 5. WHAT COMPETITORS SAY (and therefore what is *not* differentiated)

The overwhelming majority of Faisalabad-facing solar marketing says the same four things:
*"cut your electricity bill"* · *"net metering"* · *"25-year warranty"* · *"Tier-1 panels"*.
Positioning is near-identical: "leading solar company in Faisalabad", "one-stop solution",
"residential / commercial / industrial / agricultural".

Nobody credible in the Faisalabad set has published:
- an honest, plain-language explanation of the **Feb 2026 net-billing change** and what it does to a
  return calculation;
- a **buyer-protection / verification checklist** addressing the documented fraud patterns;
- a **self-consumption-first sizing tool** reflecting the new export economics;
- any **project evidence** with capacity, sector and date.

**That absence is the opportunity.** It is not a gap in decoration — it is a gap in the information
the buyer needs most at the moment of maximum confusion.
