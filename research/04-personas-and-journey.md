## PART A — WHO THE MARKET ACTUALLY IS

### A1. The addressable base is enormous and the penetration is thin — **[V]**

From the FESCO net-metered register (per-circle PDFs, published 2026-04-14):

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| FESCO net-metered connections, all divisions | 38,730 |
| Of which, Faisalabad city | **27,490 (71%)** |
| Connections ≤ 5 kW | 64% |
| Connections ≤ 10 kW | 82% |
| Industrial B2, 25–500 kW | 2,252 |
| Industrial B3, median | ~965 kW |
| Largest single connection named | Zahid Jee Textile Mills, 17 MW |

Two things follow immediately and they shape everything else.

**First, Faisalabad is not a sample of FESCO — it is most of it.** 71% of every net-metered
connection in the entire distribution company sits in one city. Any content, pricing or advisory
effort aimed at "Punjab" is, in volume terms, aimed at Faisalabad. **[I]**

**Second, the size distribution is top-heavy at the small end in count, and at the large end in
capacity.** 82% of connections are ≤10 kW, but the 2,252 B2 connections and the B3 tail hold the
overwhelming majority of the megawatts. A single 17 MW plant is 44% of the entire FESCO net-metered
fleet by itself. **[I]** This means a website that addresses only households addresses 82% of the
*count* and a negligible share of the *capacity*.

### A2. The industrial base is concentrated in four industries — **[V]**

| Industry | Units in Faisalabad |
|---|---|
| Power looms | 56,038 |
| Dyeing | 250 |
| Hosiery | 250 |
| Spinning | 137 |

Estates: M-3 (4,485 acres), AIIC (3,966 acres), Value Addition City (233 acres).

Power looms are the defining fact of this market. They are numerous, small, clustered, and they run
on a daytime-heavy profile that is unusually well matched to solar — which is precisely why 2,252 of
them have already taken net metering. **[I]** A loom unit is also the archetypal **owner-operator**
business: the person deciding is the person who will run it for the next twenty years, and there is
no procurement department between them and the invoice. **[I]**

### A3. Agriculture is the largest unmet need and the worst-served segment — **[V]**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Tubewells in Punjab | 1,088,054 |
| Tubewells in Faisalabad district | 35,019 |
| — of those, diesel | 22,384 |
| — electric | 6,493 |
| — tractor-driven | 6,142 |
| Subsidised slots available | **482** |

**22,384 diesel tubewells against 482 subsidised slots is a 46:1 shortfall.** The subsidised channel
cannot reach this market. **[I]** The only route to electrifying a tubewell here is to buy the
system. That is the single largest commercially addressable pool identified in this entire
investigation, and it is a self-pay market by arithmetic, not by preference. **[I]**

The CM Punjab Free Solar Scheme (100,000 systems, ≤2 kW, ≤200 units/month) has **closed
registrations**, and its 2 kW ceiling sits far below the 10–20 HP pump a tubewell actually needs.
There is therefore **zero overlap** between the subsidised scheme and the self-pay agricultural
opportunity. **[V]**

The CM scheme's own 100,000 systems against a 35,019-tubewell district also shows the subsidy was
never designed for agricultural load. **[I]**

### A4. The supply side is thin and formally structured — **[V]**

Certified installers in Faisalabad: **7 × C-1, 5 × C-2, 13 × C-3.**

C-3 is the ≤250 kW category, and it holds the most certified firms (13). The 2,252 B2 industrial
connections therefore compete among 13 certified bidders, while the largest loads — the B3 tail and
the 17 MW outlier — sit in C-1 with only 7 certified firms in the whole district. **[I]**

Shahzad Solar sits in C-3, i.e. in the *most* competitive band and *not* in the band that covers its
own largest published project. That specific mismatch is documented in `05` and repeated on the
site's About page. **[V]**

### A5. The macro context that is actually driving enquiries — **[V]**

- **9 Feb 2026**: SRO 251(I)/2026 replaces the 2015 net-metering regulations. The 1.5× oversizing
  permission is gone; capacity may not exceed the sanctioned load. Export is now priced at the
  national average energy purchase price (~Rs 11) instead of the full retail tariff.
- **6 Aug 2026**: SRO 1330(I)/2026 removes the NEPRA concurrence requirement for systems ≤25 kW.
- **April 2026**: the Power Division reinstated severe load shedding, roughly 2.5 h/day, 5 pm–1 am.
- Battery imports up ~220% year-on-year.
- Solar equipment GST cut from 18% to 5%.

The load-shedding window (5 pm–1 am) does not overlap the solar day at all. **Solar does not solve
the load-shedding problem; only storage does.** This is a commercially important and frequently
mis-stated point, and the site states it. **[I]**

The net effect: the regulation change made solar *less* obviously profitable for anyone with spare
capacity and export, while load shedding made storage *more* valuable. Any website that only sells
"reduce your bill" is now selling to a shrinking rationale. **[I]**

## PART B — THE FIVE VISITORS

These are **constructed archetypes, not observed individuals** **[I]**. They are built by
de-segmenting the verified numbers in Part A against the company's own published project record.
Each carries the evidence it was derived from and the specific place the site must work hardest for
them.

### P1 — THE LOOM OWNER *(largest count, industrial capacity)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Derived from** | 56,038 power looms **[V]**; 2,252 B2 industrial net-metered connections **[V]**; the company's own 250 kW Faisalabad project **[V]** |
| **Typical system** | 100 kW – 1 MW; frequently 1.0× to exactly the sanctioned load |
| **What they already know** | Net metering exists; they have probably already had one or two quotations |
| **What frightens them** | At 250 kW the new Reg 3(3) makes a **load-flow study mandatory** **[V]**. This is new, it costs money, and it is often the first time they hear of it. |
| **What they want** | A number they can put in front of a partner, and a date they can plan around |
| **What they will not forgive** | Being told approval and connection are guaranteed. They have been told that before. |

**The decisive fact for this persona is the load-flow study.** Below 250 kW it is optional; at or
above 250 kW it is required by regulation, must be done by a consultant registered with the
Distribution Board or the Pakistan Engineering Council, and it is the single most common point at
which a large quotation quietly changes price after signing. **[V]** A website that does not tell
the loom owner this *before* the quotation is not being honest, it is just being quiet.

### P2 — THE HOUSEHOLD SEEKING A SECOND QUOTATION *(largest count, smallest capacity)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Derived from** | 64% of connections ≤5 kW, 82% ≤10 kW **[V]**; the company's own ~12-panel residential rooftop project **[V]** |
| **Typical system** | 3 – 10 kW |
| **What they already know** | "Solar is cheaper now because the government allows it." Mostly from WhatsApp forwards. |
| **What frightens them** | Being sold a system larger than their sanctioned load, and then having the connection refused |
| **What they want** | A sense of whether this person is competent and honest |

This persona is not primarily price-shopping. They are **credibility-shopping**. The company's own
documented record contains a case where a small rooftop system was described as a weaving factory —
a description inflated by roughly two orders of magnitude in implied capacity. **[V]** In a market
where inflation is common, the single most commercially valuable thing a seller can do is prove
they inflate nothing. Hence the site's entire "what this does not prove" structure.

**Note the tariff trap this persona walks into.** Under SRO 251(I)/2026, capacity may not exceed the
sanctioned load **[V]**. A 10 kW system on a 5 kW sanctioned connection is now a regulatory
problem, not a clever trick. A large share of quotations circulating in Faisalabad in 2026 were
built on the old 1.5× rule and are simply wrong. **[I]**

### P3 — THE TUBEWELL OWNER *(largest unmet need)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Derived from** | 22,384 diesel tubewells vs 482 subsidised slots **[V]**; FESCO Category D agricultural tariff Rs 28.90 **[C — practitioner source, see `05`]** |
| **Typical system** | 10 HP – 50 HP pump; roughly 10–30 kW |
| **What they already know** | The tubewell scheme will probably never reach them |
| **What they will not forgive** | A claim that their electricity bill will be zero. The agricultural connection carries a **minimum of Rs 2,000 per month at zero consumption** **[V]** |

This is the persona with the strongest commercial logic and the weakest digital representation.
Every installer in the district has a "we do solar" page; almost none addresses the diesel-to-solar
conversion as a *financial* decision. **[I]**

The specific trap: solar pumps run 6–8 hours in daylight, while the agricultural tariff and the
monthly minimum are both charged regardless. The economics are usually strongly positive, but they
are never a clean substitution, and a site that implies otherwise loses the farm. **[I]**

### P4 — THE INSTITUTIONAL BUYER *(highest ticket, longest cycle)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Derived from** | The company's 150 kW hospital project **[V]**; the 1 MW paper mill **[V]**; the C-1/C-2/C-3 split **[V]** |
| **Typical system** | 150 kW – 1 MW |
| **What they already know** | How to write a tender |
| **What they want** | A counterparty that will survive a compliance audit |

Hospitals, mills and trusts are the buyers who will actually *read* the website. They are checking
whether the counterparty exists, is registered, and is currently valid. **This is the one persona
for whom the certification question is dispositive rather than decorative** — and the honest answer
available to Shahzad Solar is that its certificate date has passed and the published register is not
validity-filtered. **[V]**

The site's decision to publish that verbatim, with both category limits, rather than claim
certification, is a **bet on this persona**. The reasoning: a hospital trust that discovers the
certificate lapse during due diligence has already lost the deal and will publicise it; one that
discovers the company disclosed it itself has a reason to keep talking. **[I]**

### P5 — THE REFERRAL-BROKER REFERRER *(the real commercial channel)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Derived from** | The absence of any working website **[V]**; WhatsApp-first behaviour in the market **[I]** |
| **What they are** | Not a customer. A cousin, a loom-worker neighbour, or an electrician who sends one specific link and then gets out of the conversation |
| **What they need** | A link that does not embarrass them when the recipient opens it |

This persona explains the entire conversion design. They forward a **URL**, not a conversation. A
website that requires a form, a gate, a login, or a slow page is a website that gets deleted from
the forward and never discussed. **[I]**

The consequence is that the WhatsApp deep link must carry context. A forwarded link to a generic
"Contact us" page produces nothing; a forwarded link to a page that already understands the 1.0×
rule, the load-flow threshold, and the agricultural minimum converts. **[I]**

## PART C — THE DECISION JOURNEY, AND WHERE IT ACTUALLY BREAKS

### C1. The sequence as it really runs — **[I]**

1. **Trigger.** Either a load-shedding night, an electricity bill, or a subsidy announcement that
   creates a burst of enquiries and then a wave of disappointed buyers.
2. **Shortlist.** Two to three names, assembled from WhatsApp forwards and a Facebook page. Almost
   never from search — the buyer is not looking for a website.
3. **Contact.** A phone call or a WhatsApp message, usually at night. **No form is ever filled in.**
4. **Site visit.** The installer measures the roof or the pump.
5. **Quotation.** Delivered on paper or as a PDF. This is where the sale is usually decided.
6. **Comparison.** The buyer lines up two or three quotations and looks for the one that is
   *different in a way they can explain*.
7. **Deposit.**

**The critical observation: steps 1–3 happen before the website exists in the buyer's mind, and
step 5 is where the decision is made.** The website is not in the decision path; it is the
**artefact the decision is defended with**. **[I]**

That reframes the entire design brief. The site's job is not to generate leads. Its job is to make
the buyer *comfortable having chosen this company*, and to survive the moment they are asked
"but how do I know they're any good?"

### C2. Where trust actually breaks — **[I]**

Four failure points, in descending order of how often they occur:

| Break | Mechanism | What the site does about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Quotations are incomparable** | Different panel counts, different scope boundaries, one number each. The buyer cannot tell whether the cheap one is cheaper or just thinner. | Itemised comparison page; "how to read a quotation" checklist |
| **Everyone claims certification** | 25 certified firms in the district; the claim is nearly free to make and almost never checked | Verbatim publication of the actual register entry, including the parts that are unflattering |
| **Capacity claims are unverifiable** | A photo of a large array proves a camera was pointed at some panels | "What this does not prove" column on every project |
| **The 1.5× → 1.0× change** | Quotations built on the old rule are now wrong, and the buyer does not know to distrust them | The regulation cited verbatim, with the 250 kW load-flow threshold called out |

### C3. The specific insight that drives the whole design — **[I]**

**In this market the most valuable thing a company can publish is information that costs it
something.**

The load-flow study requirement costs the 250 kW buyer money. The certificate expiry costs the
company credibility. The "this does not prove" columns cost the company the ability to imply scale it
cannot substantiate. The corrected residential project costs the company a larger-sounding number.

Each of these is individually rational to hide. Collectively they are the only credible basis for
the one asset this business does not currently have, which is **a reputation that survives contact
with a technically literate buyer**.

## PART D — LANGUAGE AND CHANNEL

### D1. Bilingual is a requirement, not a feature — **[I]**

The company's own published contact line offers WhatsApp in Urdu, Punjabi and English **[V]**. The
buyers in P1, P3 and P5 are overwhelmingly Urdu-first. The buyers in P2 and P4 — and every
procurement process in P4 — are English.

Serving only English serves exactly the segment that was already going to buy. Serving only Urdu
serves the segment that was hardest to reach. The site therefore carries **all nine pages in both
languages**, and every page carries reciprocal `hreflang` alternates. **[V as built]**

### D2. Where the decision is actually made — **[V as built]**

Conversion is ordered by evidence, not by preference:

1. **WhatsApp** (primary) — contextual deep link, prefilled, per page. The market closes on
   WhatsApp; the site's most-used element should be a link into that conversation, not a form.
2. **Telephone** (secondary) — two published numbers, `tel:` links.
3. **Form** (tertiary) — and the form states plainly that **there is no server behind it** and
   hands the visitor a composed WhatsApp message instead. **[V as built]**

A form that silently discards submissions is worse than no form. A form that says "this is not
going anywhere, here is the button that will" is honest and still converts. **[I]**

### D3. The trust instrument — **[V as built]**

The "Before you buy" checklist: twelve checks, each with a stated *method* rather than a
statement, each applicable to the company's own quotation, persisted in `localStorage`, printable.
It is free, ungated, and explicitly licensed for the reader to reuse.

The business case is asymmetric. The checklist cannot make a buyer buy solar from Shahzad Solar. It
can make a buyer who was already leaning that way **not feel foolish** when they ask hard questions
— and in a market this small, that is most of the funnel. **[I]**

## PART F — OPEN QUESTIONS THIS FILE CANNOT ANSWER

- **[U]** What proportion of enquiries are actually agricultural. The 22,384 figure establishes the
  *installed base*, not the *enquiry mix*. Shahzad Solar's own enquiry data would settle it and is
  not public.
- **[U]** Whether the certificate has been renewed since 08-06-2025. This is the single highest-value
  unknown in the entire investigation; see `05`.
- **[U]** The SRO 547(I)/2026 text. Retrieved as an image-only scan; never read. It governs protection
  of existing connections, which is exactly the P2 question.
- **[U]** NEPRA's actual determination of the national average energy purchase price. The gazette
  fixes the formula; the resulting figure of ~Rs 11 is a Power Division statement reported in The
  News on 10 Feb 2026, not a gazette figure.
- **[U]** Whether the 2026 Punjab Solar Tubewell Scheme is open. Not published anywhere found, so the
  agricultural page says nothing about it.
- **[H]** Whether buyers respond better to a compliance-forward site or a price-forward one. No
  evidence either way was found for Faisalabad specifically. The build bets on compliance-forward.
  This is a genuine bet, not a finding.
